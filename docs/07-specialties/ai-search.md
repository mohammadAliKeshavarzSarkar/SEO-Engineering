# AI search, answer engines and emerging visibility

Google's AI search guidance keeps the existing eligibility foundations: indexed, snippet-eligible and technically accessible pages; also check the Search generative AI inclusion control in Search Console where available. Google documents this as an additional inclusion condition; do not claim settings changes or availability without property evidence. No special Google AI schema, chunking hack or llms.txt requirement. Other consumers may use optional files or crawler controls; document the actual consumer, permission policy, cost and measurable benefit before experimentation. Google AI search eligibility is not a promise of citation.

Publish original useful evidence, self-contained passages written for readers, clear entity identity, stable URLs, accurate tables and visible sources/limitations. Avoid separate bot-only copies, fabricated citations, unearned brand mentions, spam or artificial repetitive micro-sections. Make key facts available as text. For external AI platforms, verify the specific crawler purpose (search, user-initiated retrieval, training), current policy and regional availability; do not conflate them. Make allow/block decisions with content, legal and infrastructure owners.

Measure separately: bot requests (access), observed citation/grounding (visibility), measurable referral sessions (visit), and qualified conversion. No request count proves training or citations. Google documents a Generative AI performance report in Search Console. Check actual property availability, scope and current metric definitions before use; do not assume a separate report is exposed through the API or that its totals can be added to other reports without overlap analysis. Use platform-specific reports where actually available; record definitions and limitations. Preserve test query, locale, date, interface and repeatability for manual observations. Search and analytics totals are not interchangeable; avoid unverifiable AI authority scores.
