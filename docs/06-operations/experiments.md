# SEO experiments and maintenance

Record hypothesis, mechanism, cohort, baseline, primary metric, guardrails, duration/decision threshold, risks and rollback. Use comparable page groups when feasible, stagger changes and annotate releases. Avoid changing every title, URL, schema and layout at once. Search is observational and noisy; account for seasonality, updates, query mix, crawl lag and selection bias. Do not claim causality from simple before/after comparisons. Critical repairs are not optional experiments.

Measure eligibility, visibility, useful visits and verified conversion separately. Sample size and time depend on baseline volume and recrawl speed; avoid arbitrary universal time windows. Report absolute and relative results with denominators; low-data results are inconclusive. Keep negative results and superseded hypotheses. No ranking guarantee.

## Comparison validity and decision controls

Before a growth test, validate measurement stability: same intended event definition, tag/deduplication rules, consent handling, report scope, attribution model, key-event counting, reporting identity, timezone and data freshness; compare directly observed backend outcomes if approved. Annotate changes and hold causal interpretation where instrumentation changed. Record controls or comparison cohorts, allocation mechanism, staggered deployment, potential cross-cohort spillover (navigation/shared templates/canonical groups), and actual exposure to the change. User-level UX tests and page-level SEO comparisons have different units; do not present an uncontrolled page comparison as randomized user experiment. If an SEO test requires distinct URLs or redirects, follow current [Google website testing guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/website-testing) and T07; do not create crawler-only variants or imply authorization.

Choose primary outcome, analysis unit, denominator, minimum practically meaningful improvement, planned observation window/stopping rules and guardrails before inspecting outcomes. If no defensible comparison or adequate sample exists, treat results as descriptive or inconclusive. Do not claim a causal lift or significance from two aggregate percentages. Report missingness, exclusions, competing explanations, actual exposure and any protocol deviations. Do not continue sampling until a favorable result appears or change thresholds after seeing data. Safety/privacy/critical UX guardrails may trigger early hold or rollback independently of growth outcomes. An inconclusive test need not justify a full rollout. All these are internal decision practices, not prescribed SEO ranking thresholds.

Cadence (adapt): weekly during releases — errors, indexing, changes and alerts; monthly — intent/page trends, lead quality, content decay and field performance; quarterly — crawl, source updates, schema, internal links and editorial reviews; annually — architecture, policy/access and inventory. Health-sensitive content may need expert-defined shorter review intervals. Refresh actual substance, not dates only.

Automation: use official APIs within quota, paginate where supported, store extraction date and query parameters, retry with backoff, avoid PII and credentials in Git. Schedule reports only after ownership and consent approval. Use [experiment template](../../templates/experiment.md).
