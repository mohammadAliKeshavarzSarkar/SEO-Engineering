# R01 — Business objectives and search positioning

Status: initial methodology, review with project owner. Research checkpoint: 2026-11-18.

## Objective

Identify the audiences and real problems an organization can serve, the evidence it can provide, and the useful outcomes a search visit should enable. This sets the boundary for keyword research; it is not a ranking model.

## Procedure

1. Inventory actual offerings, supported features, business model, geography, language, pricing or sales process if applicable, constraints and exclusions. Distinguish current capability from roadmap.
2. Identify decision-makers, influencers, operators and end users. For each, record the situation, task/problem, desired result, vocabulary and evidence needed to choose a solution. Validate with authorized interviews, sales/support observations and product documentation. Do not invent interviews.
3. Separate category demand (users know a solution exists), problem demand (users describe an unmet need), solution comparison, implementation guidance and brand navigation. These are discovery lenses, not final SERP labels.
4. Define the primary business outcome and meaningful secondary outcomes. For lead-based sites, distinguish CTA click, form start, accepted lead and qualified lead. For commerce, distinguish product engagement, purchase and refund. For publishers, define a useful engagement outcome rather than selecting traffic alone.
5. Document each target market and locale; distinguish language from country. Identify service access, platform restrictions and measurement limitations. Do not assume global English data represents local intent.
6. Identify differentiators backed by actual proof. Map preliminary audience/problem groups to current pages or proposed research questions. A proposed page is not approval to publish.
7. Review with business/product owner; mark unresolved priorities and missing proof. Create a private decision record for objectives, audiences, geography, positioning, conversions and exclusions.

## Synthetic example

| Audience | Problem | Candidate search behavior | Useful destination | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Facility manager | Understand equipment usage | equipment monitoring system | Commercial solution overview | Accepted enquiry |
| Operator | Diagnose equipment alerts | how to interpret equipment alerts | Practical guide | Successful task / relevant solution visit |
| Buyer | Compare two monitoring approaches | sensor types comparison | Evidence-based comparison | Informed next step |

These are invented seed phrases, not measured queries or search-volume claims.

## Exit criteria

- Approved or explicitly provisional objectives, audience roles, market and primary outcome.
- Problem/solution matrix with evidence provenance and claim limitations.
- Clear out-of-scope categories and topics requiring expert review.
- At least one testable discovery question for each relevant audience.
- Baseline availability and open questions documented.
