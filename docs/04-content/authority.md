# Authority, promotion and links

Earn citations through original resources, reliable expertise, product documentation, industry collaboration, tools and genuine case studies. Build outreach around value for the recipient's audience, not exact-match anchor demands. Case studies require permission, context, methodology, limitations and real results. Do not buy links to manipulate rankings, participate in private networks, fabricate mentions or publish unrelated third-party content primarily to exploit domain authority.

Link qualification: rel=sponsored for paid placements (nofollow is also accepted by Google for paid links); rel=ugc for applicable user-generated links; nofollow where association is not intended. Use reasonable editorial links without unnecessary restrictions. Review backlinks for genuine problems; do not automatically disavow ordinary low-quality links. Disavow is exceptional and requires current platform guidance and informed review.

## Third-party publishing and reputation

Current Google documentation distinguishes exploiting a host's established search signals from legitimate guest contributions or partnerships. Evaluate real editorial control, integration with the audience's journey, origin and duplication of material, commercial disclosure and accountable ownership together. Neither a particular CMS/subfolder nor a superficial editor sign-off proves compliance. Third-party content, sponsorship or affiliate links alone do not establish reputation-policy abuse.

The reviewed site-reputation policy (2026-11-19) distinguishes search results shown inside and outside the European Economic Area (EEA): outside the EEA, relevant pages may receive manual action; within the EEA Google describes separately evaluating the affected content rather than applying that manual-action impact. This is a search-result-region distinction, not a license to move content, target the EEA or evade policy. Check current official guidance and any actual Search Console notice; never infer an enforcement event or its scope from a traffic chart. No platform or region-specific enforcement claim about a target without evidence.

## Link risk and disavow

Qualified advertising links are legitimate when handled correctly; preserve genuine editorial judgment and applicable sponsorship disclosure. Backlink vendor toxicity scores and unfamiliar domains are screening leads, not verified link-policy violations. Google's disavow guidance is exceptional: a substantial problematic link pattern and actual or likely relevant manual action must both be established before considering it. Seek appropriate removal where feasible; verify exact property and proposed list, assess impact and preserve prior records. No automatic export or submission of vendor-marked links. An authorized owner must approve any submission. Incorrect use can damage search performance. See [disavow guidance](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/2648487) and the current [site reputation policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies).

Track earned referral traffic, relevant mentions, partnerships, useful content distribution and conversion contribution. Domain authority scores are third-party estimates, not search engine scores. Review risks, editorial independence, consent and copyright before publishing.

Sources: [Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies), [qualify outbound links](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links).
