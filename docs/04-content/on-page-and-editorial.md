# On-page SEO and editorial engineering

Start with a search intent and useful answer. Unique descriptive title and primary heading should express the actual page; one clear H1 is a practical content convention, not a ranking requirement. Write specific descriptions for snippets; no universal fixed character count or guarantee that Google uses them. Titles and snippets may be rewritten. Use natural headings and terminology, descriptive links, complete definitions and relevant examples. Avoid keyword stuffing, arbitrary word counts and duplicate intent pages.

## Editorial contract

Define reader, problem, scope, unique contribution, primary sources, limitations, action and internal links in a content brief. Identify authors and accountable reviewers; use real credentials, organizational authorship when honest, evidence and corrections. Health, financial and safety claims need context-appropriate expert review. No invented experience, fabricated testimonials or unsupported product outcomes. Place citations beside important claims; distinguish measurement, observation, examples and opinion. Publish dates and update only on meaningful revisions. Maintain a correction process and contact details.

Use answer-first structure when it helps; coherent sections, tables and diagrams for comparison. Original value may be verified screenshots, field methods, qualified expert contributions, tested workflows or anonymized data with approval. AI can assist research and drafting, not substitute for evidence and accountable review. Avoid low-value scaled generation, doorway pages and repurposed domain abuse.

## Evidence and editorial acceptance

Treat E-E-A-T as a conceptual quality lens, not a measurable ranking factor or numerical authority score. Quality-rater judgments do not directly determine individual search rankings. Review trust through actual claim accuracy, demonstrated experience, appropriate expertise and accountable ownership. For health, finance and other consequential topics, require evidence appropriate to the stakes and expert consensus, not a generic author biography or schema badge. Not every useful page requires a specialist byline: match authorship and review to its real purpose. Main content may be a working tool, calculator, media or discussion rather than a long article; verify that the actual task can be completed.

For each material claim, keep a private review record: claim, primary evidence and date, applicable population/context, reviewer, uncertainty, intended visible wording and disposition (supported, qualify, defer, remove). For a product comparison, distinguish actually tested features from vendor-described features and disclose the methodology and limits. Never manufacture test results, quotes, expert profiles, synthetic portraits presented as actual authors, or customer experience. For automation-assisted content, assess whether the audience reasonably expects an explanation of the production method and give truthful disclosure where appropriate. This is not a universal requirement for a boilerplate AI label. Review any applicable legal or publication-specific disclosure requirements separately.

Automation is a production method, not an automatic spam verdict or quality credential. Approve individual task value and source reliability before scaling a template. Reject keyword variations, translations or combined summaries that add no independently useful answer when their purpose is manipulating search. A human editing pass or citation list cannot rescue invented facts or missing user value. Begin with a bounded reviewed cohort; verify real content, accessibility, original contribution and ability to maintain it before proposing expansion. Preserve private source material and author/reviewer authorization.

For refresh and retirement, classify keep, correct, substantively update, differentiate, consolidate with approved equivalence, or retire with a real removal/redirect plan. Check actual purpose, current accuracy, useful incoming links, affected users and approved replacement before retiring; low traffic alone is insufficient. Do not change publication dates or remove useful old pages merely to simulate recency. See R07 for task coverage and T07 for URL changes.

Source checkpoint 2026-11-19: [people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) and [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies). This claim-review record and bounded cohort are proposed internal acceptance practices, not prescribed ranking tests.

Publish checklist: intent answered, factual review, meaningful title/description, stable URL, accessible images, related links, mobile reading, approved CTA, sitemap and inspection. Refresh by evidence and business relevance, not cosmetic timestamps.
